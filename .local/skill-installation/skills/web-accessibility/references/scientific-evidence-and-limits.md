**Reference categories:** scientific papers below are peer-reviewed publications. They support choices about *evaluation methodology*, not vendor/framework quality, guaranteed detection coverage or legal conformance for the Ec product. Original studies were not reproduced in this package.

1. **Vigo, M.; Brown, J.; Conway, V. (2013).** *Benchmarking web accessibility evaluation tools: Measuring the harm of sole reliance on automated tests.* W4A 2013, ACM, DOI **10.1145/2461121.2461124**. Publisher-oriented metadata: https://doi.org/10.1145/2461121.2461124 ; institutional record https://ekoizpen-zientifikoa.ehu.eus/documentos/652b2a01ebee473633a38e70?lang=en . **Supported:** an empirical comparison of multiple tools on WCAG 2.0 exhibited limited coverage and completeness; using only automated tools misses issues. **Limitations:** tools, samples and WCAG version from 2013; no valid modern axe-core/2026 effectiveness percentage follows from this work.
2. **Bai, A.; Mork, H. C.; Schulz, T.; Fuglerud, K. S. (2016).** *Evaluation of Accessibility Testing Methods. Which Methods Uncover What Type of Problems?* Studies in Health Technology and Informatics 229, 506–516, DOI **10.3233/978-1-61499-684-2-506**. Publisher: https://doi.org/10.3233/978-1-61499-684-2-506 ; PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27534345/ . **Supported:** four different types of evaluation method applied to an e-ID solution revealed different and overlapping issues, with no one method finding more than half of issues in that evaluation. **Limitations:** case-study product and selection of methods; not a universal percentage for all products.
3. **Brajnik, G. (2008).** *Beyond Conformance: The Role of Accessibility Evaluation Methods.* WISE Workshops 2008, pp. 63–80, DOI **10.1007/978-3-540-85200-1_9**. Institutional abstract https://air.uniud.it/handle/11390/850823 and bibliographic index https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/wise/Brajnik08.html . **Supported:** conceptual and methodological discussion of accessibility evaluation beyond checklist conformance, including user-centered perspectives. **Limitations:** discusses methods and barriers; it does not prove any specific framework's accessibility or test effectiveness rate.
4. **de Souza, E. R.; Mont'Alvão, C. (2012).** *Web accessibility: evaluation of a website with different semi-automatic evaluation tools.* Work 41(S1), DOI **10.3233/WOR-2012-0354-1567**. Journal publication https://doi.org/10.3233/WOR-2012-0354-1567 . **Supported:** two semi-automatic tools reported differences on a government website; combining evidence sources can reveal complementary issues. **Limitations:** one website and two tools, not a general claim that any specified set of tools is sufficient.

## Derived engineering decision

Use multiple independent oracles: standards-based manual checks, automated static/scanner results, real keyboard/browser interactions, targeted screen reader evaluations and representative user testing when feasible. Log tools and environment; avoid transferring numeric effects from these studies to the current application. This decision is also directly supported by W3C WAI guidance on tool limitations: https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/tools/selecting/ and involving users: https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/involving-users/ .
